Who, what, how

Years learning how disciplined brands think. Now applied at the size where it matters most: early.

Everything you'd want to know before writing the first email: who's behind this, what each engagement actually includes, what it costs, and exactly how it runs.

It started with research, not a swatch book. My first working years were as a consumer insights researcher in Moscow — hall tests, journey mapping, competitive benchmarking, the unglamorous groundwork that told a major bank exactly why customers behaved the way they did, and drove a 15% post-launch engagement lift when the product changed to match. That habit — don't decide, find out — never really left.

It showed up next at ARKET (H&M Group), translating brand strategy into the actual customer experience for Milan's flagship store — one of Europe's largest H&M Group openings — across a team of people who'd never worked a floor together before. Getting hundreds of small, invisible decisions to resolve the same way, every time, is what got it to 98% satisfaction across 200+ daily interactions and 20% over upsell target. It's a discipline problem before it's a taste problem.

At Socialbooth, I ran that same discipline in a faster room — directing brand activation strategy for 60+ experiential campaigns for high-profile clients, holding satisfaction at 95% while cutting turnaround 20% by fixing how the workflow itself was built, not by working longer hours.

I hold an Inside LVMH Certificate in Creation & Branding and Retail Customer Experience, and studied Luxury Fashion at Libera Università Mediterranea — the formal side of a question I'd already been living: what actually makes a disciplined brand feel disciplined, mechanically, not just aesthetically.

Here's the part that isn't on a CV: almost everyone doing this kind of work well is doing it for companies that already have a marketing department to hand it to. The businesses that need a clear position most — the ones still deciding what they are — usually can't access it at all, or get a logo and a font pairing instead of an actual answer. Studio Dudin exists to close that specific gap: the same research-first discipline, aimed at the size where getting it right the first time matters most.

Process

What actually happens when we work together.

01

The first call — free, 20 minutes

You tell me what's going on, I ask sharp questions, and we figure out together whether this is a Positioning Audit, a Brand Strategy Core, or something else entirely. If it's not a fit, I'll say so — that's a five-minute conversation, not a wasted hour.

02

Scope & kickoff

Once we agree on the right package, you get a one-page agreement — clear scope, clear price, clear timeline, no legalese. A 50% deposit starts the clock; the rest is due at delivery. Then a short intake questionnaire so I'm not asking you things you've already told me.

03

The work

Research first — competitive scan, audience read, stakeholder interviews where relevant. Then synthesis: I draft the positioning, not a mood board. You'll see a working draft partway through, not just a reveal at the end — strategy improves when you can react to it early.

04

Delivery

You get actual documents, not a slide you'll open once and forget: a written positioning statement, messaging framework, and brand voice guide (per package — see Services above for exactly what's included in each). Plain language, ready to hand to a designer, developer, or your own team.

05

After

Every engagement ends with a debrief call and a 30-day question window at no extra cost — if something doesn't land right when you try to use it, that's still my problem to help solve. Want ongoing input after that? That's what Brand Advisory is for.

Before you write the email

Questions people actually ask.

Why should I trust an agency that's brand new?

You shouldn't take that on faith — that's why the Work page is honest about being new, and why I publish real, checkable audits instead of portfolio mockups. See the Socialbooth piece for what the thinking actually looks like before you pay for it.

What if I don't like the direction you come back with?

Brand Strategy Core and Brand Platform both include a draft review session before anything is final, plus at least one revision round — you react to the thinking while it's still adjustable, not after it's locked. The Positioning Audit is shorter by design, but the working session is a live conversation, not a one-way handoff.

Do you design logos or build the website?

No. Brand Platform sets the visual direction — palette, typography direction, a logo brief — so a designer or developer can execute it correctly. I set the direction; I don't produce the final assets. If you need a referral for that part, I can point you to people whose work I trust.

I'm not in Italy — can we still work together?

Yes. Everything runs over video calls and email regardless of where you are. Milan is where I'm based, not a requirement for working with me.

Can I start small and go bigger later?

Yes — starting with the Positioning Audit and expanding into Brand Strategy Core afterward is common, and I build on what the Audit already found rather than starting from zero. Exactly how that's priced if you upgrade is something we'll work out together on the call, based on how much time has passed and what's changed.

What if it's just not a fit after the first call?

Then I'll say so directly, and it costs you nothing — the first call is free precisely so that finding out isn't an expensive way to learn it.
